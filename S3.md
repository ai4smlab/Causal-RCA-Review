The quality assessment results indicate that most reviewed studies provided clear descriptions of their target systems, datasets, and evaluation procedures (Q2–Q3), supporting conceptual reproducibility and facilitating comparison across different application domains. Formal causal modeling approaches (Q1), including causal graphs, Bayesian networks, structural causal models, and causal discovery techniques, were also commonly reported.

However, implementation availability (Q5) remained limited, with only a small proportion of studies providing publicly accessible source code, datasets, or replication artifacts. In addition, validation practices (Q4) varied considerably across the literature, ranging from controlled fault-injection experiments and benchmark datasets to simulation-based evaluations and expert-driven assessments. These findings suggest that although causal root cause analysis research demonstrates generally strong methodological rigor, challenges related to reproducibility, artifact availability, and validation consistency remain important considerations when interpreting reported results and comparing approaches across domains.
